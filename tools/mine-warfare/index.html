How to read it

The grid is a generic stretch of sea, 12 km wide and 7 km deep, off a notional landing beach. It is not a real place. The PLA transport area is at the top; the beach is at the bottom.

Step 1. As Taiwan, lay mines. Each cell you paint holds a group of eight mines. How many groups you can lay depends on how many minelayers you have and how many sorties they make before the landing, which is really a question of warning time.

Step 2. As the PLA, pick a mine countermeasures (MCM) force, the hours you have, and whether to clear narrow lanes or the whole area. Shaded cells have been searched. A searched mine is usually neutralized, marked with a cross, but some are missed.

Step 3. Run the assault. Landing craft cross the field in waves along the cleared lanes or across the whole front. The panel shows expected losses, the search time the field demands, and the comparison between lanes and full-area clearance.

The core idea

Mines buy time. The attacker does not know which cells hold mines, so it must search every cell it intends to use, and most of that searching is empty water. Clearance is slow, and MCM ships work in slow, predictable patterns that a defender can see and shoot at.

Clearing lanes is much faster than clearing the whole area, which is why real clearance operations cut Q-routes, narrow cleared lanes to the shore. The price is that the assault has to funnel through a few kilometres of front, exactly where a defender would aim coastal missiles, artillery and drones. Mines channel; fires destroy.

The biggest variable sits outside the grid: how early Taiwan decides to lay. A field laid with days of warning is far denser than one laid in the last hours, and minelaying before shots are fired is a political decision with real costs to Taiwan's own shipping.

Method and notional values

  • Every numeric parameter in the simulation is notional: cell size (500 m), mines per group (8), mines per minelayer sortie (48, a middle value of the 32 to 64 range TSM cites; The Defense Post reports up to 64), MCM search rates (vessel 0.06, helicopter 0.10, UUV 0.04 cells per hour), chances a search neutralizes a group (vessel 85/80%, helicopter 80/45%, UUV 90/90% for moored/bottom mines), the 1.5× effort penalty for a mixed field, landing-craft loss chances per intact group (moored 30%, bottom 40%), MCM loss rates under coastal fire (0.8%, 0.4% and 0.2% per hour), 48 landing craft, and 12 approach columns for a full-area assault.
  • Clearance searches cells in a fixed order from seaward to shoreward. Losses are expected values: each craft's survival is the product of its chances of passing each mined cell in its column. The animation shows one seeded random draw of the same odds, so it can differ from the readout.
  • Left out: currents, weather, sand waves that bury and expose bottom mines, mine counts and ship counters, decoys and mine-like clutter, PLA preemptive strikes on minelayers, Taiwanese fires against the landing craft themselves, and the time to lay the field. Use the model to reason about the shape of the trade, not to estimate any real operation.
