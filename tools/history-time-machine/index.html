    How to read it

    Move the year slider, drag across the timeline, or press Play. The map shades Taiwan, the Penghu islands, Kinmen, Matsu, the Dachen Islands and the facing mainland coast by the government that controlled each one at the end of that year. Circles mark the small island groups so their color stays visible.

    The bands above the event dots track the arrangements that framed the Strait: who governed Taiwan, the martial-law era, the U.S. defense treaty, which government Washington recognized as China, and who held China's seat at the United Nations. Each dot is an event, colored by type. Click one to read what happened, a short quote from the document where one exists, and the source.

    Turn on Split view to put two years side by side. Places and arrangements that changed between them are outlined and listed, along with the events in between.

    Method and limits

    • The map shows control, meaning which government administered each place at the end of the selected year. It does not rule on sovereignty. Taiwan's legal status has been argued since the 1951 San Francisco treaty, in which Japan renounced Taiwan and Penghu without naming a recipient. The ROC, the PRC and the United States each describe that status differently; the quotes in the event cards give each document's own wording.
    • One controller per place per year. The mainland is shaded Qing until 1911, ROC from 1912 to 1948 and PRC from 1949. Warlord-era divisions and Japan's wartime occupation of parts of China are not shaded. That occupation included Kinmen, which Japan held from 1937 until its 1945 surrender, and parts of Matsu held by Japanese-backed forces. Kinmen and Matsu are shaded as part of Fujian before 1949.
    • Coastlines: Natural Earth 10 m land through TSM's shared map data. The Dachen Islands are missing from that data, so they appear as a marker at 28°28′N 121°54′E. Other small ROC-held islands (such as Wuqiu and Dongsha) are not drawn.
    • Band dates are the year a change took effect, with one exception: the Mutual Defense Treaty band starts when it was signed in December 1954 (it entered into force on 3 March 1955) and ends with its termination on 31 December 1979; U.S. recognition moved to the PRC on 1 January 1979; the band for U.S. recognition of the ROC starts in 1928, when the State Department recognized the Nationalist Government.
    • The event list is a teaching selection and leaves many events out. Every fact comes from the linked sources, and every quote was checked against the linked page. There are no notional values in this tool.
