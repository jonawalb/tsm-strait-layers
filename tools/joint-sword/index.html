A question to hold

Are these exercises rehearsals for using force, or performances staged for audiences in Taipei, Washington and at home? TSM's working paper Exercises as Theater (research in progress) argues they work mainly as theatrical signaling. Look at the timing of announcements, how quickly activity returns to normal and how each exercise is named and framed, and judge for yourself.

    How to compare

    Pick two or more exercises above. Day 0 is each exercise's first day in the event list. "× usual level" divides each day by the average of the 30 days before the window, so a busy baseline year and a quiet one line up.

    TSM daily aircraft data start Aug. 6, 2022. The 2020 and 2021 events have no TSM daily data, and August 2022 is only partly covered.

    How to read this

    Pick an exercise, then drag the slider or the chart to move day by day from a week before the first exercise day to a week after the last. The map shows any exercise zones whose coordinates were officially published, and marks each one as in force, announced or expired on the day you are looking at. The panel lists what was announced or said that day, with links to the reporting.

    Bars are Taiwan Security Monitor daily counts, which come from Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense. The TSM daily record starts Aug. 6, 2022 for aircraft, Aug. 7, 2022 for ADIZ entries (with some missing days) and Aug. 13, 2024 for ships, so earlier days show as "no TSM data". The panel also lists the peak figures reported at the time, with their sources.

    Compare lines up exercises on their first day so you can see how fast each one rose and fell.

    Method and limits

    • Event list, dates, triggers, forces and reported peaks: TSM's Exercises as Theater event list, where every field is backed by the linked sources below. Exercise names and wording are paraphrased from those sources.
    • Zones are drawn only where officially published coordinates were found in the announcing body's notice or in reliable reporting of it. The source is named under the map. Otherwise the map draws no zone and the announced areas are described in text.
    • Taiwan MND reports cover roughly 06:00 to 06:00 local time. TSM files each report under the day the window starts, so a day's count can include activity from early the next morning.
    • "Usual level" is the mean of the 30 days before the window (days −37 to −8), computed only when at least 10 of those days have data.
    • Counts are descriptive. They show how much activity Taiwan reported, not intent.
