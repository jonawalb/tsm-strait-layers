    How to read it

    The page opens on Countries. Pick a country to see its organization chart at the date on the slider: each box is an organization, and each card is a person with a sourced post there, with the post title. Click a card for the full profile, or Network on the card to find the same person in the network view.

    Each circle is one public official. Colour shows the country, and larger circles are tier 1: heads of state and government, defense and foreign ministers, top commanders, Central Military Commission members, theater and combatant commanders and national security chiefs. A red ring marks someone removed from office, and a dashed ring marks someone under investigation, from the date the record gives. Faded circles are people with no post at the chosen date.

    Lines are ties, and each kind can be switched on or off. Click a line, or open a tie in the profile, to see why it exists and the sources behind it. Move the As of slider to see the network at an earlier month: posts, reporting lines and status follow the date.

    Relations are sourced personal ties with a sign: a blue line is friendly (allies, patron and client, coalition partners), a red line is adversarial (rivals, opponents), and a grey dashed line is neutral or mixed. Faction lines join members of the same named faction while their memberships overlap, coloured by faction. State alignment, off by default, is a faint line coloured by the two countries' relationship. It describes the states, not the two people.

    To compare people, Shift-click or Ctrl-click them in the network, or press Compare on an org chart card or Add to comparison in a profile. Connections then draws only those people and the shortest chains of ties between each pair. The people along a chain show as small grey circles. The list beside it gives, for each pair, the direct ties by layer with sources, the length of the shortest chain and any signed relation.

    On their watch appears in the profile of anyone who held a tracked post: Eastern Theater commander and political commissar, PLA Navy and Air Force commanders, CMC chair and vice chairs, the PRC defense minister, the China Coast Guard head and the Taiwan Affairs Office director; Taiwan's defense minister, chief of the general staff and coast guard head; and the INDOPACOM commander and U.S. defense secretary. It lists TSM's record of PLA activity around Taiwan during each tenure, next to the previous and next holder. It describes what happened while someone held the post. It does not say they caused it.

    Turnover charts removals, investigations, unexplained replacements and retirements by month for one country, with a table of every case and its source. The counts at the top read "N of M": of the M people who held a post in that part of the system since the chosen year, N were removed, put under investigation or replaced without explanation.

    Meetings lines join two index people who met, spoke by phone, held talks or issued a joint statement, according to a sourced record; the line thickens with each encounter up to the chosen date. Profiles list each person's counterparts, including officials outside the index, and Connections adds a meeting matrix for the compared actors.

    Calendar looks 36 months ahead: elections, term ends and limits, party congresses, leadership contests, mandated reviews and rule-based age limits, each with its source, plus posts that are vacant or unconfirmed. Upcoming items also appear as badges in profiles. Packet, in the comparison bar, builds a printable red-cell packet with a cover and one page per compared actor.

    Taiwan legislature shows the Legislative Yuan's party blocs, a matrix of every recorded vote by legislator (Y yes, N no, A abstain, · not recorded, ! against the party majority on that vote) and an agreement network. Succession watch lists each tier-1 person's open posts with their age, time in post, any age or term rule whose wording names that rank or post, and the people now in posts that have fed it before. It is not a forecast and does not rank anyone. What changed is the log of changes to the index itself. In Connections, sanctions are drawn as red state-level ties from the imposing state to a compared actor, with a sortable table of all sanctions and a list of congressional visits to Taiwan below.

    Degree counts how many people someone is tied to in the network as filtered. Betweenness measures how often someone sits on the shortest path between two others, a rough sign of a broker. Both change with the layers, countries and date you pick, so treat them as a description of these records, not a ranking of power.

    Method

    • Career overlap: two people held posts in the same organization at the same time. The line's weight is the years of overlap. A post with no end date runs to the data date. A date given only as a year is read as mid-year, and a month as mid-month. Posts with no sourced start date are left out of overlaps.
    • Formal hierarchy: a sourced reporting line from the record's reports_to field. It shows only while the line holds at the chosen date.
    • Co-appearance: a sourced joint event, meeting or inspection. It shows from the event date on.
    • Shared background: the same home province (first part of the birthplace, same country only), the same school or academy, or the same sourced background tag. Off by default because it links many people loosely.
    • Sourcing: every post, status change, education entry, reporting line and co-appearance carries its own source URL. Official and primary sources come first; reference works such as CSIS, IISS or wire services where no official page exists. Wikipedia is never a cited source. The build drops any entry without a source.
    • Scope: public officials only, whose roles are a matter of public record. No family members, private individuals, addresses or contact details.
    • Org charts: each country chart is built from the sourced parent links between organizations and shows only people with a sourced post in that organization at the chosen date. Vacant or unconfirmed posts are left out, not filled in. Organizations with nobody sourced at that date are hidden, and former holders appear greyed with their end date when former officials are included.
    • Headshots: official portraits or Wikimedia Commons images with a stated license, credited in each profile with the author and license and linked to the file page. Images load from their source and are not copied here. People without a licensed portrait get initials.
    • Purges stay in: removed and investigated officials keep their records, with the date and source of the change.
    • Relations: each signed tie between two people needs its own https source that states it. Sign 1 is friendly (ally, patron-client, faction-mate, coalition), sign −1 is adversarial (rival, opponent), and 0 is neutral or mixed. The build drops entries with unknown people, no source or a Wikipedia source.
    • Factions: membership in a named faction needs a source per member. Two members are linked while their dated memberships overlap.
    • State alignment is not a personal relationship. It comes from sourced state-to-state dyads (treaty ally, strategic partner, rival, adversary, neutral). It is drawn only from the selected person to tier-1 leaders of other countries, or between compared actors, and only where no personal relation is recorded. It is never used for shortest paths or centrality.
    • Connections: shortest paths count steps across the person-level layers now switched on, at the chosen date. A path shows how records link people, not a channel anyone uses.
    • On their watch: tenure windows come from the sourced post dates (a year alone is read as mid-year, a month as mid-month, marked ≈) and are clipped to each series' own coverage: . Means are over days with a recorded value, and the n is shown. Joint combat readiness patrol days include days that also had a long-range flight. Named exercises are those that started inside the tenure. No tests or trends are run: activity during a tenure is not evidence that the person caused it, and posts with no sourced start date get no window.
    • Turnover: built from each person's status (removed, under investigation, replaced without explanation, retired) and its sourced date. Retirements are shown but not counted in "N of M". A case dated only to a year sits at mid-year on the chart. The family of a case comes from every post the person held, so one person can count in more than one family. For China the families are the CMC and its departments, theater commands, services and academies, the PAP and Coast Guard, and party and state bodies; elsewhere they follow organization type.
    • Meetings: each encounter is a dated, linked record of a meeting, call, talks or joint statement between a person in the index and a counterpart. Only encounters between two index people draw a line; the rest are listed in the profile by country. Line weight and the matrix count encounters up to the chosen date.
    • Tone: monthly readings of a person's attributed statements on each metric, with the metric's definition and validation status shown as given. The optional PLA aircraft overlay puts two series on one time axis for comparison only; co-movement is not evidence of cause.
    • Promotion cohort: two PLA officers promoted at the same CMC rank ceremony, from the ceremony date. Co-sponsorship: sponsor or cosponsor of the same U.S. bill or Legislative Yuan bill, from the bill date; weight is the number of shared bills. LY vote agreement: two legislators cast the same vote (yes, no or abstain) on at least 90% of at least 10 recorded votes both took part in, shown from the 10th shared vote. All three are off by default.
    • Party majority and defections: the plurality choice of a caucus's members who voted on that roll call; a tie gives no majority. Absence is not counted as a defection.
    • Calendar: scheduled and rule-based transitions with a source for each. A date given to the month or year is marked ~ and drawn as a window. Rule-based items (age and term limits) are estimates from published rules, not announcements.
    • Red-cell packet: one card per compared actor, built only from what the page holds. Ties on a card are limited to links among the selected actors. Every card carries a validation box listing what has and has not been validated; nothing in it is a forecast.
    • Crisis response ranges: the scenario section stays in validation. Two trait readings are not enough to model how a leader would respond, so it waits for the rest of the Leadership Trait Analysis and operational-code scores to pass benchmark checks. It will show a 10th to 90th percentile range per leader, never a single predicted value.
