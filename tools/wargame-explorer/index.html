          How to read it

          This tool compares published, open-source wargames about a war or coercion campaign over Taiwan. Each study appears once as a card. The matrix breaks studies into the scenario variants their authors scored separately. CSIS's First Battle project, for example, reports its base, optimistic and pessimistic runs apart.

          The five assumption columns hold what each report says it assumed. "Not stated" means the report does not say, or the question does not apply. The outcome bar splits a row's runs by how the authors scored them, colored with the key below. A row with one run is a single game played once.

          Group by an assumption to see outcomes side by side. Use the controlled comparisons for the strongest evidence: one study changing one assumption while holding the rest.

            What is not here

            • RAND. This tool found no RAND report that publishes the design and results of its own Taiwan wargame. RAND's 2024 "Denial Without Disaster" (RR-A2312-1) surveys the CSIS games rather than reporting new ones, so it is cited only as context. It describes the CSIS series as set in 2027, while the CSIS report sets it in 2026 (RAND p. 7; CSIS p. 2).
            • CSIS "Hunger Games" quarantine study. Not found under that name. CSIS China Power's 2024 quarantine study could not be opened from this build (the page returned 403) and describes scenarios rather than game results, so it is left out.
            • CNAS and CSIS follow-on games in 2025. None with published results were found beyond "Lights Out?" (CSIS, July 2025).
            • Nikkei coverage and the other JFSS simulations. The JFSS primary report is used instead of press coverage. The 1st (2021), 2nd (2022) and 4th (2024) JFSS reports are posted in Japanese on the JFSS site but were not coded here. The same page lists a joint FRA-JFSS simulation held in Taipei on August 8-9, 2026; it is not coded here either.
            • Classified games. Government wargames are not public. CSIS discusses why its results may differ from reported classified games (CSIS pp. 102-105).

            Method and limits

            • Every figure was read in the source PDF or web page listed on its card. Page numbers are the report's printed page numbers; the links open the PDF at that page. No value is estimated or filled from memory, and the tool has no notional values.
            • Outcome classes are TSM's coding of each report's own scoring, using the rules in the key. The "Lights Out?" report assigns no winners, so its rows are coded from its end-of-game electricity and import figures.
            • Assumption codes compress longer text. "Delayed (some runs)" and "Degraded (some runs)" mark CSIS's pessimistic set, where 12 of 18 runs carried that assumption and results are not reported separately.
            • The studies differ in year modeled (2025 to 2028), length of play (six days to twenty weeks), who played and how combat was resolved. A single seminar game is weak evidence; the 24-, 15- and 26-run CSIS series weigh more but share one designer team and one model.
            • Where a report contradicts itself or the press, the card says so and shows both.
            • Everything here is conceptual. No unit locations or infrastructure targets are shown.
