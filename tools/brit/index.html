How to read this

Each country is shaded by how much Chinese money it received under the measure you pick above. China itself is the darkest shade: it is the source of every flow on the map. A dark outline marks a country that has signed a Belt and Road memorandum with Beijing. A dashed outline marks a country that has left the initiative, or whose membership the source lists as uncertain.

The two measures count different things, so the map never adds them together. State lending and grants comes from AidData: loans and grants from Chinese government banks, ministries and state-owned firms to low- and middle-income countries, committed 2000 to 2021. Investment and construction comes from AEI's China Global Investment Tracker: deals of $100 million or more by Chinese companies, as equity investment or as construction contracts, in every country including rich ones, 2005 to 2023.

Click a country to open its dossier: a summary, the year-by-year pattern, the main sectors and lenders, recent reporting, and its largest projects with links to the sources AidData used to record them. Projects with a known location appear on the map as circles sized by value, and 52 large project sites, marked with dark diamonds, open a reviewed before-and-after satellite view on the map (drag the slider to compare). Clicking any other project circle opens an automatic view of its mapped location, comparing the year before the deal (2016 at the earliest) with 2025. "All project locations" shows every mapped project worldwide.

Method and limits

Totals. AidData figures use AidData's own aggregation rule: only projects it marks "recommended for aggregates" (this drops umbrella agreements and duplicates), using its adjusted amount where one exists, in constant 2021 dollars. That rule reproduces AidData's published total of $1.34 trillion. AEI figures are nominal dollars and sum investment (Dataset 1) and construction (Dataset 2) for each host country.

BRI membership. "Signatory" means a country appears on the Green Finance & Development Center's list of countries that signed a BRI memorandum of understanding (May 2025). The list's author notes that some signings cannot be independently confirmed (Austria, DR Congo, Niger and Russia are marked uncertain here) and that some memoranda may have lapsed. Italy left in 2023 and Panama in 2025. Most Chinese finance before 2013, and much of it since, was not labeled BRI at all, so the shading shows Chinese money, and the outline shows formal BRI status.

Locations. Project points come from AidData's geospatial dataset (GeoGCDF), which traces about 9,400 projects to OpenStreetMap features or administrative areas. Each point sits inside the project's mapped area; for roads, railways and multi-site programs it marks one spot along the footprint, and for projects located only to a province or district it marks that area. Projects without a location are listed in the dossier but not mapped.

AidData record links. Each project links to its page on AidData's live site, which has been updated since version 3.0: it shows amounts in constant 2023 dollars, and a few records have since been merged or retitled, so a small number of links may not resolve. Figures on this map stay with the published 3.0 release.

Coverage gaps. AidData stops at 2021 commitments, and AEI's last public release ends in 2023: newer AEI data is available only by request to its author. Neither covers small deals well: AEI records only transactions of $100 million or more (the smallest entry in its spreadsheet is exactly $100 million). Older source links in AidData's records sometimes break; dead links in each country's top projects were checked and point to an archived copy where one exists.
