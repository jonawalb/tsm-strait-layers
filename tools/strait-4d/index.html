How to read this

Each frame is one Taiwan Ministry of National Defense reporting day. Pick a crisis above to replay it, or drag along the timeline. The map shows what TSM recorded for that day where the data say where it happened: the ADIZ sectors MND named, officially published exercise zones, coast guard incidents, allied transits and cable incidents. Counts that have no location, such as the number of PLA aircraft, appear in the box at the top left and on the timeline.

Terms: the ADIZ is Taiwan's air defense identification zone, the area where it asks aircraft to identify themselves. The median line is the informal center line of the Strait. PLAN is the PLA Navy; official ships are other PRC government vessels, such as coast guard. AIS is the radio beacon ships broadcast their position on, and an MMSI is a ship's AIS ID number.

A red dot on the timeline marks a day whose count is far above its recent norm. The tool compares each day with the median of the days before it and flags it when the gap is large. That gap, in units of typical day-to-day spread, is the robust z shown in the box and the panel; +3 means three spreads above normal. You can change both the window and the threshold; they are judgment calls, not facts.

The bottom lanes of the timeline and the "What Beijing said" panel come from TSM's rhetoric corpus. They show how much PRC Foreign Ministry, Defense Ministry, Taiwan Affairs Office and state-media output mentions Taiwan each week, and how hostile the Taiwan sentences score. Official sentences are quoted; state media appears as headline and link only.

Method & sources

What each layer is
  • Daily counts (aircraft, ADIZ entries or median-line crossings, PLAN ships, official ships) and joint combat readiness patrol and long-range flight days: TSM's shared PLA series, rebuilt from Taiwan MND daily reports and press releases. Each row is one 06:00 to 06:00 MND window, filed under the day it starts. Aircraft start Aug. 6, 2022; ADIZ/median Aug. 7, 2022 with some missing days; ships Aug. 13, 2024. Flags are rebuilt from MND press releases, Dec. 2022 on.
  • ADIZ sectors: the sectors MND's English report text names for each window, as parsed for TSM's PLA Flight-Path Atlas. Sector outlines are that tool's schematic shapes. MND does not give counts per sector, so every lit sector gets the same height, set by the day's total.
  • Exercise zones: drawn only where the announcing body published vertex coordinates (Aug. 2022, Joint Sword-2023 maritime notices, Justice Mission-2025), from TSM's Exercises as Theater event list as checked for Anatomy of an Exercise. Other exercises appear as a label and in the panel.
  • China Coast Guard incidents: TSM's CGA_CCG incident tracker (complete file, rows to Sept. 2026), compiled from Taiwan Coast Guard Administration releases; CGA releases are linked where checked by hand. Pins sit at a reference point for the named area, not at the reported position.
  • Gray-zone vessel-days: TSM's AIS watchlist of coast guard and maritime militia ships, with one position per vessel per day taken from Global Fishing Watch events (loitering, port visits, fishing, encounters), counted in half-degree squares by month. Port visits at home ports are included. Global Fishing Watch data are CC BY-NC 4.0.
  • AIS zone entries: entries by PRC-flag and watchlist MMSIs into approximate zones, from TSM's own AIS collector, Sept. 4 to Oct. 1, 2026. Coverage is thin mid-Strait and east of Taiwan, and most PRC-flag traffic is civilian.
  • Allied transits: TSM's Taiwan Strait transit tracker, 2017 to 2026 (the timeline shows Aug. 2022 on). The route line is schematic.
  • Cable incidents: the sourced incidents in TSM's Undersea Cables tool. Markers show the area named in reporting, not the fault position.
  • Rhetoric: TSM's rhetoric corpus, one stream per lane so nothing is counted twice: MFA press conferences (English, 2018 on), MND (English, 2022 on), TAO (English, April 2026 on), state-media headlines in Chinese and English (Jan. 2025 to June 2026). Salience is the share of records that mention Taiwan. Hostility and escalation balance are model scores averaged over sentences that mention Taiwan. The corpus z compares a week with the stream's previous 12 weeks and is blank when volume is too low. Scores come from a sentence classifier checked against a larger model, not against human coders.
How anomalies are computed

For each series and day, the baseline is the trailing window of days with data (default 60, the day itself excluded; at least half the window must have data). The tool takes the median M and the median absolute deviation, and sets the spread s to the largest of 1.4826 × MAD, √M and 1. The √M floor treats counts as roughly Poisson so that a quiet stretch does not turn a small rise into an alarm. The day is flagged when (count − M) / s reaches the threshold (default 3). Both settings are adjustable assumptions. A flag says a day was unusual for its recent past; it says nothing about intent.

Coverage gaps and limits
  • No ship counts before Aug. 13, 2024, and no coast guard incident rows before June 2024.
  • TSM holds no carrier or individual ship positions, so none are drawn. MND's daily reports give counts, not tracks.
  • The rhetoric lanes cover different periods: TAO starts April 2026 and the state-media headline streams end in June 2026.
  • Taiwan MND's website blocks automated collectors, and the old TSM tracker site no longer serves data. This tool uses only data TSM already holds.
  • Global Fishing Watch vessel-days end Sept. 7, 2026, so September 2026 is partial.
  • The exercise list starts with events TSM has verified; exercises after Justice Mission-2025 will appear when added to that list.
