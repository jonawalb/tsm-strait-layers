    How to read it

    The crisis opens with a China Coast Guard boarding near Kinmen that Beijing calls a law-enforcement inspection. You choose a move for Taipei, then Washington, then Tokyo, then Taipei again, then Washington and Tokyo together. Beijing's replies are written into the story, so each choice changes what happens next. There are 22 situations and 7 endings.

    The ladder on the left is a simplified version of Herman Kahn's escalation ladder. Rungs light up as the crisis climbs, and the dotted trace shows the level after each move. Three lines mark the thresholds that matter most here: when military forces take the lead from coast guards, when a quarantine becomes a blockade (in law, an act of war), and when the great powers fight each other directly.

    The three meters are teaching devices. Escalation risk is how likely the crisis is to climb further. Deterrence credibility is how seriously Beijing might take Taipei's and its partners' resolve. Economic cost is the cost to Taiwan and its partners. The meters often pull against each other: the moves that raise credibility usually raise risk as well.

    Finish a path, then use Replay and compare to play again and set the two paths side by side on the ladder, the scenario map and a comparison table.

    Why Kinmen

    Near Kinmen Elsewhere (Dongsha, Penghu, east of Taiwan and others). * Partial month. Source: TSM China Coast Guard Incident Tracker.

    Method and limits

    • The scenario is invented. Its moves follow published analysis: the law-enforcement quarantine scenarios in CSIS's 2024 study, RAND's 2022 quarantine study, CSIS's 2025 blockade wargames, the Taiwan Relations Act, the U.S.–Japan Security Treaty and the San Remo Manual's rules on blockade. The 2024 Kinmen boarding and the 2024 China Coast Guard detention rules are real and cited.
    • All meter values and effects are notional. They were set by hand to show the direction and rough size of each move's effect, not estimated from data. Each meter runs from 0 to 100.
    • Beijing is not a player you control. Its replies are fixed by the path, which makes the story readable but leaves out Beijing's own uncertainty and internal politics.
    • The ladder rungs and thresholds are this tool's own simplification, not the rungs Kahn himself proposed.
    • No ships, units or locations below the level of a named island or port are modeled.
