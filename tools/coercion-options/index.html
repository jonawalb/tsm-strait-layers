How to read it

The five options run from least to most escalatory. Gray-zone pressure is happening now. A quarantine uses coast guard and customs language to control what reaches Taiwan without calling it war. A blockade uses military force to cut Taiwan off, which in the law of naval warfare is an act of war. Seizing a small outlying island is a limited use of force. An invasion is full-scale war.

The legal line matters because it shapes other governments' choices. The Taiwan Relations Act does not require the United States to defend Taiwan; it leaves the response to the President and Congress. The U.S.–Japan Security Treaty's Article V covers territory Japan administers, which does not include Taiwan, though attacks on U.S. bases in Japan would fall under it. A quarantine is designed to stay below those lines.

The radar and bar chart give each option notional scores from 0 to 10 on six dimensions. Change the assumptions in the side panel and watch them move: a committed U.S. posture raises the chance of U.S. involvement and the military risk to Beijing most for a blockade or invasion, and least for gray-zone pressure. That asymmetry is one reason analysts expect Beijing to favor options below the act-of-war line.

Selecting an option also draws its footprint on the map. The footprints are schematic zones to show the shape of each option. They are not real exercise boxes, patrol areas or landing sites.

Method and limits

  • The matrix text paraphrases the sources below, with citation numbers on each cell. The two cost judgments without a number (gray-zone pressure and island seizure) are this tool's own assessment. The legal rows summarize treaty and statute text; they are not legal advice, and the application of international law to Taiwan is itself contested.
  • All scores are notional. Each option has a hand-set baseline score on each dimension under default assumptions (ambiguous U.S. posture, no allied commitments, medium interdependence). Each assumption then shifts the scores by a hand-set sensitivity: for example, a committed U.S. posture adds 2.5 to the chance of U.S. involvement in a blockade and 0.5 in gray-zone pressure. Scores are clamped to 0–10. The full table is in data/options.js.
  • "Warning time" means how much notice defenders are likely to get, not how likely the option is. Invasion scores high because it requires months of visible preparation; island seizure scores low because it can be quick.
  • Island and port positions are approximate public locations used only for labels and schematic shapes.
