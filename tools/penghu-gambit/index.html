How to read it

Set up. In the panel, spend Taiwan's budget on a defense posture for Penghu, then choose the PLA's plan: seize the islands by assault, or blockade them and wait. For an assault, pick how many turns of preparatory strikes, which broad sector to land in, how many landing groups sail in each wave, and whether helicopters or paratroopers join.

Play. Each assault turn is 12 hours. Strikes hit Taiwan's missile batteries, air defense and drones. Landing groups cross under missile fire and through any mines, air lifts face air defense, drones hit the troops ashore, and then the two ground forces fight on the combat results table, a board-wargame chart where the strength ratio picks a column and a die roll picks the losses. The log lists every chance and every die, so you can see why each thing happened. Take the airfield intact and the PLA can fly in reinforcements; reach Magong and it wins.

Monte Carlo. One game is one roll of the dice. The Monte Carlo card replays your setup 1,000 times with the same seeded dice and reports how often the PLA holds Magong, the defense holds, or the fight ends in a stalemate. Key drivers changes one lever at a time, replays the same 1,000 games, and ranks the levers by how much they move the PLA's chance.

Outcomes: PLA holds Magong means the landing force reached Magong, or the blockaded garrison capitulated. Defense holds means the landing force was destroyed, or is pinned on the beach and outnumbered more than two to one, or the blockaded garrison still has supplies. Anything else is a stalemate.

Method and notional values

This is a teaching model in the style of a board wargame. It does not use real unit positions, beach surveys or order-of-battle data. Taiwan's assets sit in a notional zone off the map on purpose, the three landing sectors are broad areas, and the only places marked are Magong and the publicly known Magong Air Base, which shares Penghu Airport.

Only a few numbers come from open sources: the garrison of about 6,000 troops (a 2025 Taipei Times op-ed, which reported a cut from 7,185), reserves at half effect and the 30% Falklands offload penalty (both CSIS), and the facts behind each menu item: Hsiung Feng launchers and a drone force on Penghu in 2026 (Reuters), the value of stockpiles (CNAS) and of demolishing port and air facilities (RAND). Every probability, cost, rate and the combat results table are notional TSM assumptions. The table below lists them all; edit any of them in the panel and the whole page updates.

The dice use a seeded generator, so the same link replays the same game and the same 1,000 Monte Carlo games. Strength points are notional: one point is about 1,000 troops. Distances on the map are measured from Natural Earth coastlines.

Left out: the U.S. and allied response, Taiwan's air force and navy, missiles fired from Taiwan itself at the landing force (folded into a higher chance of hits in the east sector), weather and sea state, civilians, and the time needed to assemble the PLA force. Use the model to reason about how the pieces trade off, not to estimate any real operation.
