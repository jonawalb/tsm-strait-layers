How to read it

Each row is one Foreign Military Sales (FMS) case that the U.S. government has notified to Congress for Japan, and that Japan has not fully received. The bar starts on the notification date and runs to the data date, Oct. 12, 2026: its length is how long Japan has been waiting.

Bars are colored by type of capability: traditional, asymmetric or munitions. Orange sections mark cases where deliveries have started but the case is not finished; a short orange end means deliveries are documented but the date they began is not. A dashed tail runs to a reported completion date. Diamonds mark contracts, triangles mark Section 36(b)(5)(C) amendments, red ticks mark reported delays, and small dark ticks mark other milestones. Hover on a mark for a preview, and select a row for the full record and its sources.

The panel at the top is the tracker's monthly record, starting with March 2026, drawn as TSM draws its Taiwan figure and table. March to June were built retrospectively in October 2026 from dated sources. The last column gives each case's month-end status (March to June) and the dated developments that month, each with its source.

The second chart has two views. By notification date stacks today's open cases by the date each was notified, so you can see which years produced what Japan is still waiting for. It is not a history of the backlog: no monthly Japan backlog total was published before this tracker, and the tool does not reconstruct one for the years before March 2026. Tracker months shows the total in each monthly record from March 2026 on.

Method

The universe is every Japan sale or amendment notified to Congress under the Arms Export Control Act that an official page names as Japan and gives a transmittal number. Notifications come from four channels: Defense Security Cooperation Agency press releases, read through the Wayback Machine because dsca.mil blocks automated requests; the Federal Register, the only public channel for Section 36(b)(5)(C) amendments; state.gov, which took over posting notifications under Executive Order 14383 (signed Feb. 6, 2026; state.gov's list runs from Feb. 26); and the DSCA archive's Japan index pages.

Delivery status is anchored to Japan's Ministry of Defense national property register (国有財産台帳), published in the Defense White Paper's reference section as the number of main aircraft held on March 31 each year, and checked against Defense Department contract records, Ministry of Defense releases and press conferences, and press reports. The register counts aircraft held, so it can show that deliveries under a case have begun without giving a per-case count.

A case stays in the total, at full notified value, until it is delivered. Notified values are ceilings: Japan often contracts well below them. Amended cases carry their amended value, since amendments add equipment and money to the same case. Only the $1.61 billion support increment to the 2015 RQ-4 case is counted, because its three aircraft are delivered. Some older small missile cases with no public per-case delivery count are left out on age, and services cases whose period has run are left out as consumed; both are listed below with the reason.

Categories follow TSM's Taiwan tracker. Traditional weapons are "more capable and flexible than asymmetric weapons on a per-unit basis, but tend to have higher costs and require more advanced training"; asymmetric capabilities are "mobile, relatively low-cost, and counteract Chinese military advantages without emulating them"; munitions are "reloads for various types of weapons." Two calls are TSM's own: Tomahawk is filed under munitions although it also buys fire-control systems for a new counterstrike capability, and both HVGP support cases are filed as asymmetric because no U.S. weapons change hands.

Dates are those the source gives. Year-only dates are drawn at mid-year, and year-only completion at year end. Expected dates are what officials or contracts stated at the time. TSM does not forecast delivery dates.

  • Federal Register, Arms Sales Notifications naming the Government of Japan, including the amendments to the Aegis (25-0B), F-15J (23-0B), RQ-4 (25-0T) and V-22 (20-0B) cases.
  • U.S. Department of State, Arms Sales: Congressional Notifications, which replaced DSCA's postings under Executive Order 14383 in February 2026. It lists Japan's HVGP support case (TN 26-28) under March 25, 2026 and the ferry support case (TN 26-91) under Sept. 22, 2026.
  • Japan Ministry of Defense, Defense of Japan 2026 reference section, 資料9, main aircraft held on March 31, 2026 from the national property register (archived PDF); Ministry of Defense Tomahawk live-fire release and the Defense Minister's July 31, 2026 press conference.
  • Board of Audit of Japan (会計検査院): its January 2026 report found 118 FMS items worth ¥1.14 trillion still undelivered five or more years after contract, as reported by Nikkei, and expected FMS payment obligations for FY2023–25 to rise by about ¥300 billion on the weak yen, as reported by the Tokyo Shimbun. Board reports: report.jbaudit.go.jp. These figures are Japan's own count of overdue FMS deliveries; they cover contracts, not notified cases, so they are not added to this total.
  • Contracts and press: Defense Department contract records via HigherGov for the ASEV Aegis, SM-3 Block IIA and AWACS upgrade contracts; Air Force Materiel Command; Arms Control Today; Stars and Stripes; Aviation Wire; globalsecurity.org mirrors of daily DoD contract announcements. What is missing

  • No monthly Japan backlog total was published before this tracker. Its records start in March 2026; March to June were reconstructed from dated sources, and a record is added each month.
  • Per-case delivery counts for missile cases are not public. SIPRI counts by weapon type, not by case, so a missile case is marked as delivering only when a production contract, a ministry statement or a live firing documents it.
  • Values are notified ceilings, not contracted or paid amounts.
  • Most excluded cases have no sourced date for their final delivery, so they are listed below the timeline rather than drawn on it.
  • The Federal Register publishes notifications anywhere from six weeks to more than a year after Congress receives them, and state.gov's list has gaps, so recent cases can be missing.
  • The September 2026 aircraft ferry support case (TN 26-91) is carried at its notified $159 million, which may be a combined value that includes an earlier $99.9 million case below the notification threshold. If so, the new money is about $59.1 million. The Federal Register text, not yet published, will settle it.
  • Direct Commercial Sales are not covered. The ASEV's SPY-7 radars reach Japan that way, through Mitsubishi Corporation, and are not in this total.
